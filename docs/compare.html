The honest summary

Google Drive and iCloud backup are a restore mechanism, not an archive

They exist so a new phone can be made to look like the old one. That is genuinely useful, and everyone should have it switched on. But the backup is a sealed blob: you cannot open it, browse it, or pull one photo out of it. It only replaces the newest copy, so a photo deleted last month is gone from the backup too. And "include videos" is the setting people turn off first when the free tier fills up.

Export chat is a snapshot you take once and never look at again

It is the right tool for handing one conversation to a lawyer. It is the wrong tool for an archive: it caps the media it includes, has to be repeated per conversation by hand, and produces a zip that gets buried in Downloads.

Save to gallery only saves what your phone chose to download

Auto-download is off on mobile data for most people, so the holiday video someone sent while you were out is a placeholder that expires. And it fills your phone, which leads directly to the "free up space" prompt that deletes the lot.

WhatsBackUp covers a different failure — and has its own

It protects against the quiet losses: media WhatsApp deletes from its servers, a restore that skips your videos, a "free up space" tap, a phone that dies before the next backup ran. What you get is ordinary files on your own disk that keep working whether or not WhatsApp, your phone, or this project still exists.

Where it falls short, plainly:

  • It is unofficial. WhatsApp's terms do not permit third-party clients, and there is a small but real chance of an account being limited. The other three options carry no such risk.
  • It only captures while it is running. Close the app for a fortnight and that fortnight is not archived in the moment — though Import history can usually collect it afterwards.
  • It needs a Windows PC. There is no Mac, Linux or mobile version.
  • It does not restore into WhatsApp. It is an archive you read, not a backup you put back.

So which should you use?

Both. Keep Google Drive or iCloud backup switched on — that is your "new phone works" insurance, and nothing here replaces it. Add WhatsBackUp if the photos and videos themselves matter to you: the delivery proof, the damaged part, the signed page, the pictures of the kids that only ever existed in a chat.

They fail in different ways, which is exactly why having both is worth the five minutes.
