Where your data goes

Short version: onto your own disk, and nowhere else — unless you switch on one specific feature, in which case we will show you exactly what it sends before it sends it.

There is no service behind this

No account to create. No server we operate. No analytics, no crash reporting, no usage statistics, no licence check, no "phone home". The app talks to WhatsApp Web and to nothing else of ours — because there is nothing of ours to talk to.

Your files are just files

Photos and videos land in folders you choose, as ordinary JPGs and MP4s. Message text goes into a standard SQLite database on the same disk. Nothing is encrypted into a format only we can read, and nothing stops working if this project does.

What the app stores on your PC

  • Photos and videos from your chats, in the folders you picked during setup.
  • Message text, sender, timestamp and chat name, in a local SQLite database.
  • The WhatsApp link itself — a browser profile that is your linked-device session. Treat it like a password: anyone with that folder and the app could read your messages.
  • Your settings, and a rotating log capped at a size you set.
  • If AI sorting is on: the labels and album names it produced, in the same database.

All of it lives under %LOCALAPPDATA%\WhatsBackUp\ unless you chose somewhere else for your media. Uninstalling the app leaves every bit of it in place.

The one thing that can leave your PC

AI sorting is the only feature that sends anything anywhere, and it is switched off until you deliberately turn it on.

When you enable it, WhatsBackUp sends your photos and a sample of your message text to the AI provider you choose, using your own API key. There is no intermediary: the request goes from your PC to that provider directly.

Before the very first call, the app shows you the literal payload it would send — one photo and one chat, exactly as they would appear in the request — and you have to accept it. You can look again at any time from ⚙︎ Settings → AI sorting → Show me exactly what leaves my PC.

What is actually sent

  • For a photo: the image itself, the name of the chat it came from, whether you sent or received it, and its caption if it had one.
  • For a conversation: the chat name and a sample of the message text — the 30 most recent messages plus 30 spread across its history, trimmed to 200 characters each. Not the whole conversation.
  • Never sent: your API key to anyone but the provider you configured, your media files to us, or anything at all when the feature is off.

Or keep it entirely on your machine

Choose Ollama or LM Studio as the provider and the AI runs on your own PC. Nothing crosses the internet, no key is needed, and it costs nothing. The app will not even send an authorisation header to a local model — there is no secret it needs.

There is also a Demo mode that makes up plausible labels locally, so you can see what sorting looks like without any provider at all.

Your API key

Encrypted with Windows' own data protection (DPAPI) and stored as ciphertext. It is never written to the settings file in readable form, never appears in the log, and is deliberately stripped from the diagnostic report you might copy and email when asking for help. It is decrypted in memory only to make a request to the provider you chose — and only ever sent to the address you saved.

Other people's data

This is the part most backup tools skip. WhatsBackUp archives media from everyone you chat with, and none of them agreed to that. Their photos, their voice notes if you enable them, their words.

Keeping a personal copy of messages sent to you is generally lawful in most places, but that changes when you republish them, or when you hold other people's personal data in a business context — the GDPR and equivalents elsewhere apply to you, not to us. Keep the copies to yourself, delete what you should not keep, and use the per-chat exclusions in settings for conversations that should never be archived.

Removing everything

  • AI labels and albums: ⚙︎ Settings → AI sorting → Delete all AI labels and albums.
  • The WhatsApp link: ⚙︎ Settings → Connection → Unlink, and remove the device from your phone's Linked devices list.
  • Everything else: uninstall the app, then delete %LOCALAPPDATA%\WhatsBackUp\ and your media folder. Nothing is left anywhere else, because there is nowhere else.

Changes to this page

If the app ever starts sending something it does not send today, this page changes and so does the notice inside the app — before the feature ships, not after. The wording here is kept deliberately in step with the consent screen you actually see.

The unavoidable caveat

  • This is not an official WhatsApp app. It connects the way WhatsApp Web does, using an open-source library WhatsApp does not endorse. WhatsApp's terms do not permit unofficial clients, and there is a real if small chance an account gets limited or banned for using one.
