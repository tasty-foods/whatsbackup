Frequently asked questions

Everything people ask before installing a program that reads their messages — answered plainly, including the parts that do not flatter us.

The basics

How do I automatically save WhatsApp photos to my PC?

Install WhatsBackUp on Windows and scan one QR code with your phone. From that moment every photo and video you send or receive is written to a folder on your PC automatically, with nothing further to do. A single click also imports media already sitting in your existing chats.

The link uses WhatsApp's ordinary Linked devices feature — the same one WhatsApp Web uses.

What exactly is WhatsBackUp?

A free, open-source Windows desktop app that archives your WhatsApp media and message text to your own PC. It runs quietly in the system tray, saves photos and videos as ordinary files, keeps message text in a searchable local database, and can mirror videos into a cloud folder you already sync.

It can also sort your archive with AI — albums for photos, projects for conversations — but that part is optional and off until you switch it on.

What does it save?

Photos, videos and message text by default, from one-to-one chats and groups, both sent and received. In settings you can also switch on documents, voice notes, audio files and stickers, or narrow it to only what you want — received only, images only, specific chats excluded.

Does it cost anything?

No. It is free and open source. No account, no subscription, no storage limits, no upsell.

The only thing that can ever cost money is the optional AI sorting, and only because it uses your API key with a provider you choose. Sorting a library of a few hundred photos costs roughly $1.50 once, the app quotes the figure before spending anything, and there is a monthly limit you set. Choose a model running on your own PC via Ollama or LM Studio and it costs nothing at all.

Which Windows versions does it work on?

Windows 10 and Windows 11, 64-bit. There is no macOS, Linux, Android or iPhone version. Your phone can be either Android or iPhone — that side works through WhatsApp's standard linked-device feature.

Privacy and safety

Does it upload my messages anywhere?

No. There is no account, no server of ours, and no telemetry of any kind. Your media goes into folders on your PC, and into your own cloud folder if you set one up.

The single exception is AI sorting, which is switched off until you deliberately enable it. When on, it sends photos and a sample of message text to the AI provider you pick, using your own API key. Before the first call the app shows you the literal data it would send, and you have to accept it. Pick a local model and nothing leaves the machine at all.

Where is my API key stored?

Encrypted with Windows' own data protection, on your PC only. It never appears in the settings file in readable form, never in the logs, and never in the diagnostic report you might send when asking for help.

Will this get my WhatsApp account banned?

There is a real if small risk, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. WhatsBackUp uses an unofficial client library. WhatsApp's terms do not permit unofficial clients, and accounts have been limited for using them.

In its favour: it behaves like an ordinary linked device, sends no messages, does no bulk operations, and automates nothing that looks like spam. But the risk is not zero. If your WhatsApp account is essential to your livelihood, weigh that up before installing.

Is it legal to archive messages other people sent me?

That depends on where you live and what you do with them. Keeping a personal copy of messages sent to you is generally fine in most places; republishing them, or holding other people's personal data in a business context, can fall under privacy laws such as the GDPR.

What we can tell you plainly is that the app archives media from everyone you chat with, and none of them agreed to that. Keep the copies to yourself, and delete what you should not be keeping.

Why should I trust an unsigned program that reads my messages?

You do not have to. That is the honest answer, and it is why the entire source code is public. You can read exactly what it does, and build the installer yourself with npm install and npm run dist instead of trusting the binary we built.

If reading thousands of lines of someone else's code is not realistic for you, that is a completely fair reason to decide against installing this — or any tool like it.

Using it

Can it get back photos I have already lost?

Sometimes. Import history downloads media still present in your chats, which is frequently years' worth of photos people assumed were gone.

But WhatsApp removes older media from its servers, and once a file is gone from both WhatsApp and your phone, nothing can bring it back — this app included. Anything unavailable is reported as skipped rather than quietly missed. This is exactly why it is worth running before you need it.

Does my PC have to stay on?

It only captures while it is running. WhatsApp delivers to connected devices, so anything arriving while the app is closed is missed in the moment — though Import history can usually collect it afterwards.

Let it start with Windows and sit in the tray, and this stops being something you think about.

Can I back up WhatsApp to pCloud, OneDrive or Dropbox?

Yes. Point it at any folder your cloud client already syncs — pCloud Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox, Google Drive for desktop, or a mapped network drive — and videos are written straight there as they arrive, so your cloud client uploads them without keeping a second copy on your disk. If the drive goes offline, files are kept locally until it comes back.

How does this compare to WhatsApp's own chat export?

Export chat is a one-off snapshot; this is a standing archive. WhatsApp's export must be repeated for every conversation by hand, caps how much media it includes, and hands you a zip file nobody opens again. WhatsBackUp covers every chat at once, runs continuously, saves full-resolution media as ordinary files, and keeps everything searchable.

There is a side-by-side comparison if you want the detail.

What happens when WhatsApp changes something and it breaks?

The app notices and tells you. WhatsApp updates its website without warning, and that can stop capture dead — it has happened before, and it will happen again. WhatsBackUp counts consecutive download failures; three in a row and the tray icon turns amber, a notification fires and a banner appears in the app.

That matters more than it sounds: the worst failure is the silent one, where you find out months later that nothing was saved. Keep automatic updates on, so the fix reaches you.

What happens to my files if I uninstall it?

They stay exactly where they are. Every photo, video and message it saved is an ordinary file in an ordinary folder, and the message database is standard SQLite. Nothing is locked inside a proprietary format, and nothing depends on this app continuing to exist.

AI sorting

What does the AI sorting actually do?

It turns a date-ordered pile into something you can navigate. Photos are grouped into albums it names itself in the language of your own content — Deliveries, Machine repairs, Paperwork — and conversations are grouped under the project or subject they are actually about. Photos also become searchable by what is visible in them, including text read off an invoice, a label or a screenshot.

Which AI providers can I use?

Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, OpenRouter, Groq, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint — and Ollama or LM Studio for a model running on your own PC, which needs no key, costs nothing, and sends nothing over the internet.

Whatever you pick, Test connection asks it three separate questions first: whether the key and model name are real, whether it can follow a strict format, and whether it can actually see images. A model that cannot see images will still sort your conversations rather than failing hundreds of times over.

What does AI sorting cost?

Around $1.50 for a library of a few hundred photos and a couple of hundred conversations, using the default model. The app shows you the estimate before spending anything, and results are cached — a second run only pays for what is new. There is a monthly limit you set, and it stops when it is reached.

With a local model through Ollama or LM Studio, the cost is zero.

What if it puts something in the wrong album?

Fix it once and it stays fixed. Rename an album, move a photo, or take one out entirely, and the next sort is told about your correction and works around it instead of undoing it. That was the hardest part to build and it is the part that makes the feature worth having.

Can I try the sorting before paying for anything?

Yes — pick "Demo mode" in the provider list. It labels and groups your library locally with no key, no internet and no cost, so you can see exactly what sorted looks like before deciding. Delete all AI labels and albums clears it afterwards.
