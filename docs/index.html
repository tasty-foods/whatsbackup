The quiet problem

Your photos are already disappearing

Not dramatically. A few at a time, in ways nobody notices until the day they go looking.

  • ✕WhatsApp deletes media from its servers. Open an old chat and the photo is a grey box saying it is no longer available. It is not on your phone either, because you never opened it in time.
  • ✕A new phone loses what the backup missed. Restores skip media, run out of Drive space, or quietly fail. People discover this on day one of the new phone.
  • ✕"Free up space" is a trap. The prompt that clears 8 GB of photos does not ask which ones mattered.
  • ✕Exporting a chat is not a backup. WhatsApp's own export caps the media it includes, has to be repeated per chat, and gives you a zip nobody ever opens again.
  • ✕The proof you need is in a chat. The delivery photo, the damaged part, the signed page, the meter reading — scrolling two years back on a phone to find one picture is nobody's idea of an archive.
  • ✕Nothing is searchable. Thousands of files named IMG-20240817-WA0043.jpg in one endless date-ordered pile.

How it works

Three steps, then forget about it

WhatsBackUp links to your phone exactly the way WhatsApp Web does — by scanning a QR code once. After that it just runs.

  1. Install and open it

    One installer, no prerequisites. No Node, no Chrome, no developer tools, and no administrator password — it installs for your user only.

  2. Scan the QR code once

    On your phone: WhatsApp → Settings → Linked devices → Link a device. The same flow you already use for WhatsApp Web.

  3. Everything is saved from then on

    Every photo and video you send or receive lands in your folder the moment it arrives. One click also imports what is already sitting in your chats.

Optional AI sorting

A pile becomes an archive

Left alone, any backup is one long list in date order. Switch on AI sorting and WhatsBackUp reads what it has saved: photos land in albums it names itself, and conversations group under the project or subject they are actually about.

  • Search a photo by what is in it — including text it reads off an invoice, a label or a screenshot.
  • Rename an album or move a photo and it stays that way. Your corrections teach the next sort instead of being overwritten by it.
  • Bring your own API key: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, OpenRouter, Groq — or run it free and fully offline on your own PC with Ollama or LM Studio.
  • It quotes the cost before spending anything. A few hundred photos is roughly $1.50, and there is a monthly limit you set.

What you get

Built to be left running

The whole point is that you stop thinking about it. That takes more than a download button.

Everything, automatically

Photos and videos you send and receive, in one-to-one chats and groups, saved the moment they arrive — with the message text stored in a searchable local database.

Straight into your cloud

Point it at a pCloud, OneDrive, Dropbox or network folder and videos are written there directly, so your existing cloud client syncs them off the PC without a second copy.

Find the one you need

Filter by chat, by date, by sent or received. With AI sorting on, search by what is actually pictured — and by text read straight off the photo.

Catches up on history

Import history walks your existing chats and pulls in media WhatsApp still holds — often years of photos you assumed were gone.

Tells you when it breaks

WhatsApp changes its website without warning. If capture starts failing, the tray icon turns amber and the app says so — instead of failing silently for a month.

Yours alone

No account, no server of ours, no telemetry, no upload limits. Ordinary files in ordinary folders that stay working even if this project disappears tomorrow.

Before you download

Three things you should know

You are about to let software read your messages. You deserve to hear the awkward parts from us rather than find them later.

Read this properly

  • This is not an official WhatsApp app. It connects the way WhatsApp Web does, using an open-source library WhatsApp does not endorse. WhatsApp's terms do not permit unofficial clients, and there is a real if small chance an account gets limited or banned for using one. If that risk is not acceptable to you, do not install it.
  • It saves other people's media too. Everything sent to you gets archived, and the people who sent it never agreed to that. Keep the copies to yourself and delete what you should not keep.
  • It has to be running. WhatsApp only delivers to a connected device, so anything arriving while the app is closed is missed — though Import history can usually pick it up afterwards.

Questions

The ones people actually ask

Does it cost anything?

No. WhatsBackUp is free and open source, with no account, no subscription and no upload limits. The only thing that can ever cost money is the optional AI sorting, which uses your own API key with the provider you choose — roughly $1.50 for a library of a few hundred photos, or nothing at all if you run a model locally.

Does it upload my messages anywhere?

No. There is no account, no server of ours and no telemetry. Files go to your folders and your own cloud folder if you set one. The single exception is AI sorting, which is off until you switch it on, shows you the exact data before the first call, and can be pointed at a model running on your own PC so nothing leaves the machine.

Will this get my WhatsApp account banned?

It is a real if small risk, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. WhatsBackUp uses an unofficial client library, which WhatsApp's terms do not permit. It behaves like a normal linked device and does not send messages, spam or automate anything — but the risk is not zero. If your account is critical to your livelihood, weigh that up before installing.

Can it get back photos I have already lost?

Sometimes. Import history pulls in media that is still in your chats, which is often years' worth. But WhatsApp removes older files from its servers, and once a photo is gone from both the servers and your phone, no tool can bring it back — this one included. That is exactly why it is worth running before you need it.

Does my phone need to stay connected?

Yes, in the same way WhatsApp Web does. WhatsBackUp appears in your phone's Linked devices list and receives messages while it is running. Leave it running in the tray — it starts with Windows if you let it — and everything is captured as it arrives.

Why is Windows warning me about the installer?

Because it is not signed with a paid certificate — that costs several hundred euros a year, which a free tool does not have. Windows SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC" for anything it does not recognise; it says nothing about whether the app is safe. Click More info → Run anyway. If you would rather not trust a stranger's binary, the full source is on GitHub and you can build it yourself.
