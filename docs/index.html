Windows 10 & 11 · Free & open source

Everything went through WhatsApp. Nothing came out of it.

The quote, the address, the photograph of the damage, the delivery note, the yes — every one of them arrived in a chat and stayed there, scrolling a little further out of reach each day. WhatsBackUp keeps all of it on your own disk, then gathers the conversations into projects and the photographs into albums, so picking a job back up means opening one project instead of searching three chats and a phone.

§ 01 The losses

Six ways your photographs leave without telling you

None of them look like a disaster at the time. That is precisely why nobody acts until they go looking for something and it isn't there.

  1. 01

    WhatsApp deletes media from its servers

    Open a chat from two years ago and the photo is a grey box: no longer available. It isn't on your phone either, because you never opened it in time.

  2. 02

    A new phone loses what the backup missed

    Restores skip media, run out of cloud space, or quietly fail halfway. People find this out on day one of the new phone, which is the worst possible day.

  3. 03

    "Free up space" doesn't ask which ones mattered

    One tap clears eight gigabytes of photographs. It does not distinguish between a meme and the only picture you have of your grandmother's kitchen.

  4. 04

    Exporting a chat is not an archive

    WhatsApp's own export caps the media it includes, must be repeated conversation by conversation, and hands you a zip file that gets buried in Downloads.

  5. 05

    The proof you need is buried in a chat

    The delivery photo, the damaged part, the signed page, the meter reading. Scrolling two years back on a phone to find one picture is not an archive, it's an ordeal.

  6. 06

    Nothing is searchable

    Thousands of files called IMG-20240817-WA0043.jpg, in one undifferentiated pile, ordered only by the date they happened to arrive.

§ 02 Setting up

Three steps, then you can forget about it

It links to your phone exactly the way WhatsApp Web does — one QR code, once. After that it simply runs.

  1. 01

    Install it

    One installer, nothing to set up first. No Node, no Chrome, no developer tools, and no administrator password — it installs for your user alone.

  2. 02

    Scan the code

    On your phone: WhatsApp → Settings → Linked devices → Link a device. The flow you already know from WhatsApp Web.

  3. 03

    Walk away

    Every photo and video you send or receive lands in your folder as it arrives. One click also pulls in what is already sitting in your chats.

§ 03 Making sense of it

A record you can actually work from

Any backup left alone is a list in date order, which is barely better than the phone it came from. Switch sorting on and the archive reads itself: conversations gather under the job they belong to, photographs fall into albums it names, and the whole thing becomes searchable by what is in it rather than what it was called.

§ 04 Speaking back

Your status, kept up without you

Everything so far reads. This is the one place the app is allowed to speak: it can post your WhatsApp status on a schedule, from material you control — and nothing else, to nobody in particular, ever.

On a schedule you set
Daily at nine, Mondays at eight, every six hours. A slot that was due while the PC slept posts on wake if it is still timely, and says so in the history when it isn't.
From four kinds of source
A queue you fill by hand — any photo in the gallery is one click from it. A drop folder, where saving a file is scheduling it. An album the sorting built, so a Products album becomes a rotating storefront. Or written fresh by the AI from your standing instructions.
Text that looks designed
Text statuses are set on a 1080×1920 card — brand gradient, dark, newsprint, poster red — rendered on your PC and posted as an image. The history keeps the exact card that went out.
Hard to switch on by accident
Its own consent notice in plain words. Dry run by default: everything runs except the send, and the history reads "would have posted" until you deliberately go live. A daily cap, a minimum gap, quiet hours.

§ 05 What it does

Built to be left running

The whole point is that you stop thinking about it. That takes rather more than a download button.

Everything, as it arrives
Photos and videos you send and receive, in one-to-one chats and groups, saved the moment they land — with message text kept in a searchable local database.
Straight into your cloud folder
Point it at pCloud, OneDrive, Dropbox or a network drive and videos are written there directly, so your existing client syncs them off the PC without a second copy.
Catches up on history
Import history walks your existing chats and downloads the media WhatsApp still holds — frequently years of photographs you had assumed were gone.
Says so when it breaks
WhatsApp changes its website without warning. Three failed downloads in a row and the tray icon turns amber and the app tells you — rather than failing silently for a month.
Posts your status for you
Scheduled statuses from a queue, a drop folder, a sorted album or an AI writer — behind its own consent, dry-run first, capped and logged. How it works ↑
Plays without opening anything
Videos show a frame in the grid and play silently as your pointer crosses them. Quick play runs them one after another, full screen, the way a feed does — sound on when you want it.
Finds the one you need
Filter by chat, date, sent or received. With sorting on, search by what is actually pictured, including text read off a label or an invoice.
Yours, and only yours
No account, no server of ours, no telemetry. Ordinary files in ordinary folders that keep working if this project disappears tomorrow.

§ 07 Disclosure

Three things that don't flatter us

You are about to let a stranger's software read your messages. You should hear the awkward parts from us, not discover them later.

  1. This is not an official WhatsApp app. It connects the way WhatsApp Web does, through an open-source library WhatsApp does not endorse. Their terms do not permit unofficial clients, and there is a real if small chance an account gets limited or banned for using one. If that risk is unacceptable to you, don't install it.
  2. It archives other people's media too. Everything sent to you is kept, and none of the senders agreed to that. Keep the copies to yourself, and delete what you shouldn't be holding.
  3. It only captures while it is running. Anything arriving with the app closed is missed at the time — though Import history can usually collect it afterwards.

§ 09 Questions

What people ask first

Q1

Does it cost anything?

No. Free and open source — no account, no subscription, no limits. The only thing that can ever cost money is the optional sorting, which uses your own key with the provider you choose: roughly $1.50 for a few hundred photographs, or nothing at all with a model on your own PC.

Q2

Does it upload my messages anywhere?

No. No account, no server of ours, no telemetry. Files go to your folders and your own cloud folder. The single exception is AI sorting, which is off until you switch it on, shows you the literal data before the first call, and can be pointed at a model on your own machine so nothing leaves it.

Q3

Will this get my WhatsApp account banned?

It is a real if small risk, and we won't pretend otherwise. It uses an unofficial client library, which WhatsApp's terms don't permit. It behaves like an ordinary linked device, sends no messages and automates nothing — but the risk isn't zero. If your account is critical to your livelihood, weigh that first.

Q4

Can it get back photographs I've already lost?

Sometimes. Import history pulls in whatever is still in your chats, often years of it. But once a file is gone from both WhatsApp's servers and your phone, no tool recovers it — this one included. Which is the entire argument for running it before you need it.

Q5

Why is Windows warning me about the installer?

Because it isn't signed with a paid certificate — several hundred euros a year that a free tool doesn't have. SmartScreen shows that warning for anything it doesn't recognise; it says nothing about whether the software is safe. Click More info, then Run anyway. Or read the source and build it yourself.
